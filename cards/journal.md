# The Journal of E. Ashworth

->*Found among the effects of the Ashworth Estate, 1947*<-
->*Donated to the Blackwood Historical Society by persons unknown*<-

## Day One

[[margin(bottom, type: absolute)]]
{
I have arrived at the manor.
}
 The journey from [[cite(https://www.britannica.com/place/London)]]{London} was [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{unremarkable} exhausting in a way I cannot adequately describe. I recall boarding the train at King's Cross. I recall the rhythmic clatter of wheels on track, the grey countryside sliding past the window like a dream half-remembered. I recall closing my eyes for what I believed to be a moment.

When I opened them, the train had stopped. The conductor informed me this was my station. I do not recall the name he spoke. The platform was empty save for a single carriage, black as a hearse, the driver's face obscured beneath a wide-brimmed hat. He did not speak. He did not need to. He knew where I was going.

[[margin(left, type: relative, orient: vertical, size: 280)]]
{
[[style(font-weight: bold; color: #2c3e50)]]{October 12th, 1892}

[[anchor(initial-resolve)]]{I have resolved} to keep a detailed account of my time at Ashworth Manor. A [[cite(https://www.scientificamerican.com, Scientific Method)]]{man of science} does not succumb to fancy. He observes. He documents. He draws conclusions from evidence.

The solicitor's letter was vague. I do not recall any relatives in this county. I do not recall responding to any advertisement.

*I must have signed it.*

*A man does not simply acquire property.*

*The ink on my signature looked fresh. Wet, almost. As if I had just signed it. As if I was still signing it. As if somewhere, in some room I cannot remember, my hand is still moving across the page, writing my name over and over and over—*

**I must have signed it.**

*Why can't I remember signing it?*

*Why can't I remember anything before the train?*

He stopped the carriage at a bend in the road where the trees grew thick and the light seemed to fail despite the hour. When I asked why, he simply pointed toward the manor and made a sound that might have been laughter or might have been a sob. I could not tell. I did not ask again.

[[anchor(gates)]]{The gates} were already open when I arrived. Wrought iron, twisted into patterns I could not quite follow—shapes that seemed to move when viewed from the corner of one's eye. The solicitor left provisions in the kitchen and a brief letter on the study desk. The letter mentioned "previous arrangements" and "the usual terms." It referenced conversations I do not remember having, agreements I do not recall making. It thanked me for my "patience during the transition" and wished me well in my "return."

Return. As if I had been here before. As if I was coming back to something rather than arriving for the first time.

I have never been to this house. I am certain of this. Almost certain. Though I wonder about [[jump(groundskeeper)]]{the previous caretaker}.

## [[anchor(day-two)]]{Day Two}

I slept poorly, though I cannot say why. The bed is comfortable—more comfortable than any I have known. The linens smell of lavender and something else, something older, something that reminds me of [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{her} hospitals. The room is warm despite the season. And yet I woke repeatedly throughout the night, convinced each time that someone had just left the room, that the door was just closing, that footsteps were just fading down the corridor.

Looking back now, I realize the signs were there from [[jump(day-two)]]{the very beginning}.

[[margin(left, type: relative, orient: horizontal, size: 300)]]
{
*[[anchor(dream-sequence)]]{He slept fourteen hours. He dreamed of water.} He dreamed of hands reaching up from darkness, fingers pale as bone, grasping at nothing, grasping at everything, grasping at him. He dreamed of a woman's face beneath the surface, eyes open, mouth open, screaming without sound. He dreamed of her hair spreading like ink, like blood, like the dark between stars. He woke with his pillow damp. He woke with her name on his lips—a name he does not remember, a name he cut out of himself like a tumor, a name that lives in the walls now, in the water, in the spaces between his heartbeats.*

[[style(color: #7f1d1d)]]{He told himself it was sweat. It was not sweat. It was her. It is always her. It will always be her.}
}

I explored the ground floor today. The house is well-maintained, though I have seen no servants. The floors are swept, the surfaces dusted, the fires laid and ready for lighting. In the kitchen I found fresh bread cooling on the counter, though I had heard no one enter, no one baking, no one at all.

The [[anchor(library)]]{library} is extensive. Thousands of volumes line the walls, many of them old, some of them very old indeed. I found several that predate the house itself—medieval manuscripts in languages I do not recognize, illuminated texts with illustrations that seem to move when the candlelight flickers. I have resolved not to open these. Some knowledge is better left undisturbed.

But the [[jump(books-know)]]{books seemed to know things} about me that I had forgotten.

[[margin(right, type: relative, orient: vertical, size: 320)]]
{
**[[anchor(books-know)]]{THE BOOKS KNOW THINGS.}**

I found one today—leather-bound, no title on the spine. When I opened it, the pages were blank. Every one of them, blank.

But the margins were filled with handwriting. *My* handwriting.

*"Do not trust the mirrors."*

*"[[anchor(well-warning)]]{The well is not empty}."*

*"She is still here."*

*"She never left."*

*"She is in the water and the water is everywhere."*

*"She is in the walls."*

*"She is in your lungs."*

*"You breathed her in the moment you arrived."*

*"You will never breathe her out."*

I burned the book. I watched it turn to ash.

*It is back on the shelf now.*

*The pages are no longer blank.*

*They are filled with her name.*

*Over and over and over.*

**[[anchor(eleanor-name)]]{ELEANOR ELEANOR ELEANOR ELEANOR}**

*See [[jump(dream-sequence)]]{what he dreams} and [[jump(well-warning)]]{what the well contains}.*
}

In the study I found the previous owner's effects: letters tied with black ribbon, a collection of pressed flowers (dead now, brown and crumbling at the edges), a mirror turned to face the wall. I did not turn the mirror around. Something in the way it had been placed—hastily, desperately, the cloth draped over it torn as if in urgent haste—suggested that whoever had done so had good reason.

I also found [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{a journal} nothing else of note. The letters I have not read. The flowers I have left undisturbed. The mirror I will not touch.

## Day Three

[[margin(top, type: absolute, orient: horizontal)]]
{
**I HAVE NOT SLEPT IN TWO DAYS — THE FOOTSTEPS DO NOT STOP — SHE IS LOOKING FOR ME**
}

Something is wrong with the west wing. The corridor that leads to it is perfectly ordinary—wallpapered in a faded floral pattern, lit by windows that let in the grey afternoon light, furnished with occasional tables and forgettable paintings of forgettable landscapes. And yet I cannot bring myself to walk down it.

I have tried three times now. Each time I reach a point approximately halfway down the corridor and find myself unable to continue. My legs do not fail me. My courage does not falter. I simply... stop. As if I have reached an invisible barrier. As if my body knows something my mind does not.

[[margin(left, type: relative, orient: vertical, size: 260)]]
{
*His body remembers.*

*Even if his mind has chosen to forget.*

*The body always remembers.*

*The body remembers the weight of what we carry.*

*The body remembers the stain on our hands.*

*The body remembers the water in our lungs.*

*The body remembers the cold.*

*The body remembers the dark.*

*The body remembers her face looking up through the water, eyes wide, mouth open, bubbles rising—*

*The body remembers the hands that held us under.*

[[style(color: #7f1d1d; font-weight: bold)]]{His hands. His hands held us under. His hands pushed us down. His hands kept us there until the bubbles stopped. Until we stopped. Until everything stopped except the waiting.}

*We have been waiting so long.*

*We are so tired of waiting.*
}

The west wing is [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{locked} [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{forbidden} [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{where it happened} perfectly accessible. I simply choose not to go there. A man has preferences. A man has instincts. This is normal. This is rational.

I have been hearing things at night. Footsteps in the corridor outside my room. A woman's voice, too soft to make out the words, coming from somewhere below. The creak of a door opening and closing, opening and closing, as if someone were checking each room in sequence, searching for something. For someone.

Last night I woke to find my bedroom door open. I am certain I locked it before retiring. I am certain of very little else, but I am certain of this. I checked the lock three times. I tested the handle. I [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{barricaded it with a chair} went to sleep secure in the knowledge that no one could enter.

The door was open. The chair was back at the writing desk, positioned precisely where it had been when I first arrived. And there were wet footprints on the floor—small, delicate, a woman's footprints—leading from the door to my bedside and back again.

[[style(background: #fef2f2; padding: 20px; border-left: 4px solid #991b1b)]]
{
I followed the footprints to the window. They did not stop at the door. They went to the window, which was also open, the curtains moving in a breeze that should not have existed—the night was still, I remember the stillness—and below the window, in the garden, I could see a figure standing by the well.

A woman in white. Looking up at me.

By the time I reached the garden, she was gone. But the cover of the well had been moved. Just slightly. Just enough.
}

## Day Five

[[margin(right, type: relative, orient: horizontal, size: 340)]]
{
*The letters are his. He wrote them. He wrote them in the summer of 1889, when he still loved her, when he still knew how to love, before the jealousy ate through him like acid, before the suspicion became certainty, before certainty became rage, before rage became murder. He cut out her name himself, one careful rectangle at a time, trying to forget, trying to erase her from the record of his life. But you cannot erase what the house remembers. You cannot cut out what lives in the walls, in the water, in the very stones. She kept every letter. She kept them tied with the ribbon from her wedding dress—the dress she was wearing when he pushed her, the dress that billowed around her as she fell, the dress that wrapped around her like a shroud as she sank. She kept them even after. Even after the splash. Even after the silence. Even after three years at the bottom of the well, she kept them, and now they have found their way back to him, and he will read them, and he will remember, and he will come to us.*

[[style(color: #7f1d1d)]]{He will come to us. He has no choice. He never had a choice. The gates were always open. The well was always waiting. We were always here.}

I have not slept. I cannot sleep. Every time I close my eyes I see her—the woman by the well, her face always turned away, her white dress moving in a wind I cannot feel. She is waiting for something. For someone.

The letters. I broke down and read the letters today. They are love letters, written in a hand I do not recognize to a woman whose name has been carefully excised from every page. Neat rectangular holes where her name should be, cut with scissors or a knife, the edges yellowed with age. The letters speak of devotion, of passion, of a love that transcends death itself. They speak of Ashworth Manor as a sanctuary, a place where two souls might be together forever, away from the judgment of the world.

They are signed with a single initial: E.

I told myself it was a coincidence. Many names begin with E. Edward. Ernest. Edmund. Eugene. It means nothing. It proves nothing.

But the handwriting. The handwriting is [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{familiar} [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{mine} similar to many samples from the period.

I found a photograph pressed between the pages of the final letter. A woman in white standing before the manor. She is beautiful—dark hair, pale skin, eyes that seem to look directly at the viewer no matter what angle you hold the photograph. She is smiling, but there is something wrong with the smile. Something strained. Something desperate.

On the back of the photograph, in handwriting that matches the letters, a single line: *"Eleanor, Ashworth Manor, Summer 1889."*

Eleanor. [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{I know that name} The name means nothing to me.

Yet I have seen it written [[jump(eleanor-name)]]{over and over} in my own hand.

## Day Seven

[[margin(top, type: absolute, orient: horizontal)]]
{
->**THE VOICE IN MY HEAD IS NOT MY OWN — IT SPEAKS WITH WATER IN ITS THROAT — IT KNOWS MY NAME**<-
}

I am beginning to understand that I am not alone in this house. I do not mean ghosts—I am a man of science, I do not believe in ghosts—but there is a presence here, a consciousness that exists alongside my own, that watches me from the shadows, that whispers to me in the moments between waking and sleeping.

It knows things. It knows things about me that I do not know myself. It speaks in a voice that is almost familiar, almost my own, but twisted somehow, filtered through water and darkness and three years of waiting.

Perhaps one day I will [[jump(final)]]{understand} what it wants from me.

[[margin(right, type: relative, orient: vertical, size: 300)]]
{
[[style(color: #7f1d1d; font-style: italic)]]{He says he is alone. He says he has always been alone. He says he does not remember.}

*But we remember.*

*We remember the summer of 1889.*

*We remember the wedding in the garden—the white dress, the flowers, the vows spoken beneath the oak tree that still stands, that has watched everything, that knows what he did.*

*We remember the honeymoon. The villa in Italy. The way he looked at her like she was the sun.*

*We remember when the sun went dark.*

*We remember the arguments. The accusations. The letter he found—her sister's letter, asking if she was safe, asking if the rumors were true, asking if she needed help escaping.*

*He read it as betrayal.*

*He read it as proof.*

*He read it as permission.*

*We remember the walk to the well.*

*We remember her asking where they were going.*

*We remember his hands on her shoulders.*

*We remember falling.*

[[style(color: #7f1d1d; font-weight: bold)]]{We remember the water closing over our head. We remember the cold. We remember the dark. We remember looking up and seeing his face, watching, waiting, making sure. We remember the last bubble leaving our lips. We remember dying.}

*We remember everything.*

*And soon, so will he.*
}

The handwriting in my journal is changing. I compared the early entries to today's. The script grows more angular, more hurried, more desperate. And in the margins—in the margins there are notes I do not remember writing. Observations in a hand that is mine but not mine. Accusations. Confessions.

I went to the well today. I do not know why. I found myself there as if my feet had carried me without my mind's consent, standing before that sealed stone cylinder with my hands pressed against the iron bands, feeling the cold seep into my palms.

[[margin(left, type: relative, orient: horizontal, size: 280)]]
{
**[[anchor(wedding-ring)]]{I found a wedding ring today.}** It was in my pocket. I do not own a wedding ring. I have never been married. I am certain of this. *I am certain.* The ring is gold, worn thin with years of wearing. The inside is engraved: *"E & E, until the waters take us."* E & E. Edward and [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{Elizabeth} [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{Emma} [[style(text-decoration: line-through)]]{I don't remember I don't remember I don't remember} Eleanor. *The waters take us.* The waters. *Take us.* **The well is full of water.** *I don't remember any Eleanor.* **I DON'T REMEMBER.** *Then why am I crying? Why can't I stop crying? Why does the ring fit perfectly? Why does it feel like it has always been there? Why does my hand feel naked without it? Why do I keep hearing her voice? Why is she calling my name? Why won't she stop? Why won't she ever stop?*
}

The well has been sealed for a long time. The stones that cover it are thick, heavy, mortared in place. The iron bands are rusted but solid. Whatever is down there—if anything is down there—cannot get out.

And yet.

I heard something. A sound from beneath the stones. A tapping. A scratching. A voice, too muffled to make out the words, but unmistakably a voice. A woman's voice.

She was saying my name.

But how could she know it? How could she know about [[jump(wedding-ring)]]{what I found}?

[[style(background: #1a1a2e; color: #eee; padding: 24px)]]
{
I ran. I am not ashamed to admit it. I ran back to the house and I locked every door and I sat in the study with a bottle of brandy and I did not move until dawn.

But I could still hear her.

Through the walls. Through the floor. Through the very foundations of this house.

Calling my name. Over and over. Patient as stone.

*Edward. Edward. Edward.*

*It's time to remember.*
}

## Day Nine

I have stopped sleeping entirely. Every time I close my eyes I see the well. Every time I drift toward unconsciousness I feel hands around my throat—small hands, delicate hands, a woman's hands—and I wake gasping, clawing at the sheets, certain that I am drowning.

[[margin(right, type: relative, orient: vertical, size: 340)]]
{
*He's starting to break.*

*The walls he built around his memories—brick by careful brick, mortared with denial and sealed with years of deliberate forgetting—they're cracking.*

*We can see through the cracks now.*

*We can slip through.*

*We are slipping through.*

*Soon he'll remember the argument—the breakfast table, the letter in his hand, his voice rising, her tears falling, her protests ignored.*

*Soon he'll remember the accusation—the letter he claimed proved her unfaithful, the letter that was nothing more than a sister's concern, a sister who knew, a sister who tried to save her.*

*Soon he'll remember the rage—the red behind his eyes, the roaring in his ears, the way his hands shook, the way his heart turned to stone.*

*Soon he'll remember her face as she realized what he intended to do—the confusion becoming fear, the fear becoming horror, the horror becoming acceptance.*

*She knew. At the end, she knew. She looked into his eyes and saw nothing she recognized.*

*The man she married was already gone.*

*Only the monster remained.*

[[style(color: #7f1d1d)]]{Soon he'll remember the water closing over her head. The hands holding her down. The bubbles rising. The silence after. The terrible, absolute silence. And then, slowly, the sound of water settling. Of something sinking. Of the last light leaving her eyes.}

*Soon.*

*So very soon.*

*We have waited three years.*

*We can wait a little longer.*

*But not much longer.*

*We are so hungry.*
}

The mirrors have started showing me things. I covered them all on the third day—every mirror in the house, draped with sheets and cloths and whatever fabric I could find—but I can feel them watching me through their coverings. And sometimes, when the light is wrong, I can see shapes moving beneath the sheets. A figure standing where my reflection should be. A woman in white, her face obscured, her hand raised as if reaching for me.

I looked. God help me, I looked. I pulled the sheet from the mirror in the study and I looked.

[[margin(left, type: relative, orient: vertical, size: 260)]]
{
*I saw myself.*

*But older. Decades older. Grey-haired and hollow-eyed and wearing clothes I do not own—clothes that are wet, dripping, covered in algae and mud and things that grow in dark water.*

*And behind me, in the reflection, she was there.*

*Eleanor.*

*She was standing right behind me. Her hand on my shoulder. Her face pressed against my neck. Her lips moving against my ear.*

*I could feel her breath. Cold. Wet. Smelling of deep water and rotting flowers and three years of patient decay.*

*She was whispering something.*

*Over and over.*

*The same words.*

*"Until the waters take us."*

*"Until the waters take us."*

*"Until the waters take us."*

*I spun around and there was nothing. No one.*

*But when I looked back at the mirror—*

*When I looked back—*

*She smiled.*

*Her teeth were black.*

*Her eyes were empty.*

*Her smile went on forever.*

**I HAVE COVERED ALL THE MIRRORS. I WILL NOT LOOK. I WILL NOT LOOK. I WILL NOT LOOK I WILL NOT I WILL NOT I WILL**
}

I tried to leave today. I packed my case. I walked to the gates—those gates that are always open, always waiting. I stood on the threshold, one foot on the manor grounds and one foot on the road beyond.

I could not move. Not because something stopped me—there was no force, no barrier, no invisible hand holding me in place. I could not move because I did not want to. Because some part of me, some deep and terrible part that I have spent three years trying to silence, knows that I belong here. That I have always belonged here. That I came back not because of any letter or inheritance or legal document, but because *she called me home.*

{
The gates are always open.

They are open because they are waiting.

They are waiting for those who belong here.

And I belong here.

*I have always belonged here.*
}

## Day Eleven

[[margin(top, type: absolute, orient: horizontal)]]
{
**I WENT TO THE WEST WING — I REMEMBER EVERYTHING — GOD FORGIVE ME I REMEMBER**
}

I woke in the west wing. I do not remember walking there. I do not remember opening the door that I have avoided for eleven days. I do not remember climbing the stairs to the room at the end of the corridor—the room that has been calling to me since I arrived, the room that I knew, with a certainty beyond reason, I must never enter.

But I woke there. On the floor. In the dark. My hands covered in something wet that might have been water or might have been something else entirely.

[[margin(left, type: relative, orient: horizontal, size: 320)]]
{
I remember now. I remember everything. The summer of 1889. Our first summer as husband and wife. The way she laughed when she saw the manor for the first time, how she called it "our castle," how she ran through the rooms like a child, touching everything, claiming everything, making it ours. I remember being happy. I remember loving her. I remember the exact moment when loving her became fearing her—fearing that she would leave, fearing that she loved another, fearing that I was not enough, would never be enough, could never be enough. I remember the letter. Her sister's letter. Asking if Eleanor was safe. Asking if the rumors were true. Asking if she needed help. I remember reading it as betrayal. I remember the rage. I remember the walk to the well. I remember her asking where we were going, her voice uncertain, her hand in mine. I remember telling her I wanted to show her something. I remember her trust. Even then, even afraid, she trusted me. [[style(color: #450a0a; font-weight: bold)]]{I remember my hands on her shoulders. I remember pushing. I remember her face as she fell. I remember the splash. I remember the bubbles. I remember waiting until they stopped. I remember walking back to the house alone.} **I remember the silence.**
}

The room was a bedroom. *Our* bedroom. Mine and Eleanor's. The bed was made, the linens fresh, as if someone had been expecting us. Her things were still there—her brush on the vanity, her clothes in the wardrobe, her jewelry box on the nightstand. Three years and nothing had been touched. Nothing had been moved. Nothing had been allowed to change.

[[margin(right, type: relative, orient: vertical, size: 280)]]
{
*He's in our room now.*

*The room where we spent our wedding night, where he held me and told me he would love me forever, where I believed him, where I was foolish enough to believe him.*

*The room where I waited for him on nights when he worked late, when he drank too much, when he came home smelling of whiskey and suspicion.*

*The room where I cried myself to sleep, wondering what I had done wrong, wondering why his love had curdled into something dark and possessive and afraid.*

*He's holding my hairbrush.*

*He's touching my things.*

*He's crying.*

[[style(color: #7f1d1d)]]{He should cry. He should weep oceans. He should drown in his tears the way I drowned in the water.}

*But tears are not enough.*

*Tears are never enough.*

*Tears cannot bring me back.*

*Tears cannot undo what was done.*

*Tears cannot fill the emptiness where my life used to be.*

*Only he can do that.*

*Only his life can fill that emptiness.*

*And he will give it to me.*

*He will.*

*He has no choice.*

*He never had a choice.*
}

On the vanity, propped against the mirror she used to sit before each morning, I found a photograph. The same photograph I had found in the letters—Eleanor in white, standing before the manor—but different. This one was taken later. Her smile was gone. Her eyes were hollow. And in the reflection of the window behind her, barely visible, a figure. A man. Watching her.

I know that figure. I know the set of those shoulders, the angle of that head, the hands clasped behind the back.

It is me.

[[style(background: #0a0a0a; color: #f5f5f5; padding: 24px)]]
{
I remember that night. The walk through the garden. Her confusion turning to fear as we approached the well.

*"Edward, what are we doing here? Edward, please, you're hurting me. Edward—"*

I remember the silence after. The terrible, absolute silence. And then, slowly, the sound of water settling. Of something sinking. Of the last bubbles rising to the surface and breaking.

I remember standing there for hours. Waiting. Waiting for her to climb out. Waiting for her to forgive me.

She never climbed out.
}

## Day Thirteen

I have opened the well.

The stones were heavy. The iron bands were rusted but yielded to the crowbar I found in the gardener's shed. The mortar crumbled like ash. It took me hours—the entire morning and most of the afternoon—but I opened it.
